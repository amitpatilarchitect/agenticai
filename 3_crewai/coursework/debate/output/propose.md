I strongly support the motion that there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs.

LLMs are not just harmless tools; they are powerful systems that can influence what people believe, how they work, and even how they make life-changing decisions. When a technology has that much reach, leaving it largely unchecked is irresponsible. Strict laws are necessary to ensure safety, accountability, and public trust.

First, LLMs can spread misinformation at massive scale. They can generate convincing but false content, deepfake-style text, fraudulent messages, and manipulative propaganda in seconds. Without legal limits, bad actors can use them to scam people, deceive voters, and exploit vulnerable users. A system this powerful must be governed before harm becomes normal.

Second, LLMs can reinforce bias and discrimination. They are trained on human data, which means they can inherit and amplify prejudice in hiring, policing, education, healthcare, and finance. If companies are not legally required to test, audit, and correct these systems, unfair outcomes will be hidden behind a veneer of neutrality. Strict regulation is the only way to force transparency and accountability.

Third, the speed of innovation does not excuse the absence of safeguards. We regulate medicines, aircraft, food, and financial systems because the potential harm is too great to trust solely to private companies. LLMs belong in the same category of high-impact technology. If a model can affect millions of people, it should not be deployed on a company’s goodwill alone.

Finally, strict laws do not mean banning innovation. They mean setting clear rules: mandatory safety testing, transparency about training data and limitations, independent audits, liability for harm, and strong protections against abuse. Good regulation does not kill progress; it makes progress trustworthy.

In short, LLMs are too influential, too easily misused, and too important to leave unregulated. Strict laws are not an obstacle to innovation — they are the foundation for responsible innovation.
